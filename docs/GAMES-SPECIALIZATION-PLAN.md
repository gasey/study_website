# Games specialization plan

Planning only · 24 October 2026 · implementation priorities and acceptance contracts.

Students must be able to practise and compete within their specializations. The first choice is an explicit subject or topic within the launch exam scope. Mixed subjects remain an intentional option. This document proposes future behavior; it does not report a deployment, data backfill or new verification results.

## Selection and pool contract — P0

Keep the underlying dimensions independent:

1. **Exam-source scope:** default `mizoram-recruitment`, displayed as “MPSC & Mizoram recruitment”. Source-paper metadata decides inclusion. A canonical question needs a matching appearance in the chosen scope; its displayed source must come from that scope too. Unassigned imports are excluded. An eventual “All exams” means the union of explicitly enabled exam scopes, deduplicated by canonical ID, never every stored paper.
2. **Reviewed classification:** select one subject, then optionally topic and subtopic. Use approved primary and related paths. Subject/topic/subtopic constraints must match the **same path**, preventing an unrelated secondary subject and primary topic from forming a false intersection. Ancestor selection includes reviewed descendants; missing topic data is eligible for its approved subject only. Validate active registry IDs and parent relationships server-side.
3. **Game rules:** mode, question count, timing and scoring policy remain explicit. Classification means subject/topic/subtopic only. Difficulty labels, selectors, inference, authoring and scoring remain retired.

Library shelves such as Geography may resolve to topic paths under General Studies rather than top-level subjects. Reuse their approved saved-query mapping, freeze the resolved paths/version, and label it accurately. Do not invent a new subject taxonomy or treat personal note tags, paper titles, parked model alternatives or material tags as question classification.

First visit has no selected specialization and no enabled Start until the student chooses. A deliberate “Mixed subjects” option must state its scope. Remember the last choice as an account-bound preference, displayed on return and revalidated; a Library deep link can prefill its exact selection. Changing or clearing a parent clears incompatible descendants. Scope changes require recounting. Existing historical mixed rooms remain resumable.

Build one server eligibility function used by facets, preview, room creation and cup preparation. It must retain every current draw restriction and strict `official`/`key` provenance through the scoped `v_scorable` pool. Never substitute inferred, derived, unkeyed or community questions to fill a game. Counts are distinct **usable** canonical questions after source and presentation checks. Facets expose usable count, required count and unavailable reason for each mode, including zero/underfilled selections. Avoid `ORDER BY random()`; retain application ID draws and primary-key fetches.

| Mode | Required usable distinct questions | Initial rules retained |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Gauntlet | 20 | 25 seconds each; three lives; distance/streak score |
| Speed | 10 | 15 seconds each; absolute 60-second run |
| Duel | 10 | Shared questions; 20 seconds each; two players |
| Private multiplayer | 10 | Shared questions; 25 seconds each; 2–4 players |
| Cup track | 25 | Shared event set; 30 seconds each; existing weekly window |

These are hard completeness thresholds, not claims of a sufficiently diverse competitive bank. Preserve full-length rules initially. An eight-question topic cannot silently become a ten-question mixed game or contain repeated IDs. Show “8 usable verified-key questions; 10 needed” with explicit actions to broaden to its parent, choose another subject, or open clearly labelled Library practice. Do not change the chosen scope automatically. Start rechecks availability transactionally; stale previews receive a structured conflict with refreshed counts and no stranded membership/reward receipt.

## Fair play by mode — P1/P2

| Surface | Proposed behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Solo | Run only the confirmed selection. Show personal results grouped by the same mode, rules version and selection; label differently drawn runs as personal practice, without claiming equivalent exams or cross-subject rank. Retain last-ten history wording until real pagination exists. |
| Private duel | Host freezes selection before issuing the code. A code opens a minimal pre-join rules preview; joining accepts the context. Start only after both participants acknowledge that exact context. No keys, question IDs, participant directory or answer-bearing previews. |
| Private multiplayer | Same preview/acceptance; 2–4 players, host starts after all current participants are ready. A changed setup requires a new room; a newly joined player must acknowledge. Recheck membership/blocks/state under the existing locks at start. |
| Random duel | Match exact normalized context: exam scope, reviewed selection, mode, count, timings and rules version. Never pair separate specialties or silently widen a topic. Retain oldest eligible waiting-room order, bilateral blocks and one-active-game constraints. Offer Cancel, play solo, private code, or **explicitly choose** a broader queue after a wait. Keep the current ten-minute expiry and do not invent opponent counts, bots or wait-time estimates. |
| Weekly cups | Keep the existing event explicitly labelled “Mixed subjects cup” while specialization work ships. Later introduce a small scheduled catalog of curated subject/topic tracks with 25 usable questions each; choose a track before entry. Every entrant in a track receives identical frozen questions/order/timing. Do not offer arbitrary personal filters within a ranked cup. Rank each track independently. |

Custom mixtures are P2, after a single-path release. Accept at most three disjoint reviewed selections with explicit integer quotas summing to the mode count; show them before start. Reject overlapping ancestor/descendant or alias selections initially. If related paths still overlap, assign each canonical to only one quota using a deterministic allocation and check all quotas together; raw per-topic counts alone do not establish feasibility. Fail with the underfilled sections instead of silently reweighting. An explicit broad mixed-subject draw may be bank-weighted; disclose that it is not a balanced exam blueprint. Cup mixtures require a published quota blueprint. Students with different specialties may voluntarily choose a shared parent or agreed mixture; the host never gives each competitor a different paper and compares raw scores.

Cup tracks retain one entry **across the entire weekly event per account**, including forfeits, to prevent entering several tracks for extra attempts. Separate event identity from track identity before changing the current `(cup_id,user_id)` uniqueness contract. Do not relabel prior cups or migrate historical entries into invented tracks. If a track fails preflight, mark it unavailable; never borrow another track's questions. No cross-track overall winner, prizes, paid entry or automatic ability rating in this work.

Preserve current score rules: normal games use 100 plus up to 50 server-timed bonus per correct answer; Gauntlet uses distance. Publish tie rules alongside each rules version; normal competitive ties use score then existing server elapsed metric, exact ties share rank. No browser timestamps or client score claims. Do not call network-timed bonuses a latency-neutral measure. Shared rooms must apply one exclusion set to all members. Cups need one event-track validity ledger and provisional standings until close so a key/source correction between entries excludes the same positions for everyone. Freeze original personal results; expose a separate final cup assessment rather than rewriting historical room scores. Until this exists, retain and disclose the current correction limitation instead of claiming new fair track rankings.

## Context, rewards and safety — P0/P1

Introduce an additive versioned `game_context` snapshot and normalized `context_hash` on rooms; track snapshots need the same contract. Include resolved exam scope IDs, selection kind/paths and labels, shelf/taxonomy revision or fingerprint, mixture quotas, strict eligibility policy version, mode/count/timers/scoring/tie policy, and cup event/track identity where relevant. Hash canonical normalized IDs/rules, not request ordering or cosmetic labels. Snapshot the selected question IDs, scoped appearance identities and membership reasons with the existing private question payload. Expose only safe human-readable context before play.

Protect every context/rules field with a database immutability constraint, extend start-request hashing to include it, and use the hash for matchmaking and history grouping. Freeze explicit scope membership before drawing. Reconnect and retries must return the original context even if a user preference, taxonomy label, shelf, paper scope or bank entry later changes. A removed approved classification or source-scope assignment during a live game makes affected questions ineligible under the new completion policy; apply shared exclusions consistently and record the reason. Relabelling without membership change does not alter the frozen display. Never reconstruct old missing context as if historically known; show “Legacy mixed/subject game” using only stored facts.

Continue the existing canonical reward claim and shared daily cap; changing mode, topic, related path, scope, opponent, cup track or rematching cannot reset eligibility. No XP for queue time, invitations, wins alone or repeated canonical answers; no prize/coin economy. Private rematches are useful practice but do not create new claim opportunities. Preserve server proof, exactly-once finalization, released active slots, forfeiture rules and answer/discussion gates. Add bounded create/join/queue abuse controls through existing write guards, with transparent retry messages; do not infer cheating from a specialization or a fast correct answer alone. This is not external-assistance or multi-account anti-cheat certification.

Keep private room codes and existing audience/block/sanction rules. Room preview must require a valid code/account, reveal only game rules/count/expiry and use rate-limited generic unavailable responses for expired/blocked rooms. Do not expose a browseable room/participant directory or send unsolicited invitations. Recipient-bound game invitations via existing mutual/private Desk relationships are a separate later packet: explicit sender action, recipient acceptance, expiry/revocation, bilateral blocks and generic in-app notices. Desk discovery/classes remain off; email delivery remains deferred. A game link must not add Desk membership or share private progress.

## Accessible flow and useful return visits — P1

Put “What would you like to practise?” before every start action, including featured Gauntlet. Show scope → subject/shelf → optional topic/subtopic → game mode → concise rules/count confirmation. Provide labelled keyboard-operable controls, visible focus, 44px targets, status/error text beyond color, stable layout and readable path breadcrumbs. On small screens, stack controls and keep the chosen context visible in lobby, play and result headers. Preserve answer layout/source context and reduced-motion preferences; timers must not trigger screen-reader announcements every second. Explain timing before entry and offer an untimed Library practice exit. Any future extended-time competitive format needs its own disclosed rules/queue rather than silently changing one participant's clock.

Show active game/mock resume above setup; an unfinished mock must continue through its existing runner/submission flow. Preview, start failure and expired code retain the user's selection. Auth changes clear private lobby/history/preference state and invalidate pending requests. Cancellation and Leave remain usable when blocked or the opponent disappears. No forced social signup beyond current registered-account play.

For learning retention, result actions are “Review answers”, “Practise this topic” and “Play again with these settings”. Cup review unlocks only after close. Rematches use a fresh room and both players' consent; avoid recent questions where possible, without relaxing eligibility or claiming novelty in a small pool. Show honest “Some questions may repeat” feedback. Compare personal progress within its context, include excluded denominators, and avoid global rankings over unrelated specialties. Saved preferences, short sessions and actionable review are the initial return-visit features; daily streak penalties, push reminders and new reward multipliers are outside scope.

For data retention, do not mistake room expiry for deletion. Proposed later operational packet: remove join secrets from terminal rooms after a short documented recovery window; retain minimum immutable context/result/reward-proof records needed for review and claim integrity; define access, account deletion/anonymization and bounded operational telemetry retention before collecting new analytics. Existing immutable event/cup triggers and foreign keys require an explicit migration/legal-hold design for any purge. Do not attach a cleanup job that deletes attempt, reward or audit proofs. No game retention schedule or full erasure guarantee is delivered by this plan.
