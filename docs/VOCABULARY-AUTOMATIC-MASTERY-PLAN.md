# Vocabulary: automatic mastery and review scheduling

Planning only · 24 October 2026. No implementation, migrations, tests or production verification are claimed here.

**Recommendation:** replace required Again/Hard/Good/Easy ratings with a versioned, deterministic scheduler driven by server-checked first answers and successful reviews separated in time. Save the answer and next review together, then show feedback and a single **Next** action. Start with accurate evidence of recognition in the existing MCQs; do not claim a selected option proves independent recall or display an invented confidence percentage. The first engine requires a free account, keeps server-owned schedules and leaves old local ratings untouched. No language-model call or trained prediction model is needed for the first complete release.

**Review of the implementation direction supplied during planning:** account-only scheduling, one server-issued card per account/scope, immutable presentation/key proof, atomic review-attempt/schedule receipts, a ten-minute repair step and a 1/3/7/14/30/60-day ladder are appropriate. Treat one promotion per IST day as an additional ceiling, not proof of spacing: 23:59 and 00:01 are different dates but only two minutes apart. Require the due-time/exposure gap too. Skip may defer ten minutes but must not be classified as failure or reset mastery. Serialize a shared canonical-item projection across scope-specific cards. Free login and leaving old browser records untouched are acceptable first-release boundaries; guest scheduling/import can follow separately.

## Evidence model: what the engine can conclude

Use a learning item key `(canonical_question_id, task_kind, content_version)`. Initially `task_kind=mcq_recognition`; `content_version` fingerprints the exact meaning-bearing question/options/key/instructions/passage and selected source presentation. Keep the stable canonical ID for attempts and future reviewed deduplication. A word string alone is not an item ID: meanings, antonyms, synonyms and contextual uses differ. Do not merge two questions because they share a word, automatically transfer success to every related topic, or call a question count a count of mastered words.

Record these independent facts, without deriving a single psychological “confidence” value:

| Observation | Permitted use |
| --- | --- |
| Server-checked first choice against a valid verified snapshot | Correct/incorrect answer evidence for that item and task. A correct choice can still be a guess; do not identify it as a guess or certainty. |
| Successful first choice at a due review after a real time gap | Incremental evidence of retained recognition; can increase the interval by one step. |
| Wrong choice or explicit “I don’t know — show answer” | Bring the item back sooner. A reveal has no selected answer and must not be stored as a fabricated wrong attempt. |
| Answer revealed, hint used, source opened after answer disclosure, or a recent same-item check | Exposure/assisted practice; no delayed-mastery promotion. Viewing choices is intrinsic to recognition and is not an additional hint. |
| Multiple tries after feedback | Practice only. Keep the first answer result; retrying options cannot turn it into a clean correct event. |
| Skip, closed tab, network failure, interruption or no answer | No correctness judgment. Preserve/reschedule availability without a lapse penalty. |
| Optional active response duration and timing quality | Pace context only in v1; never infer wrong, guessed, careless or mastered from speed. |

Read existing recent attempts as exposure evidence where their timestamps are reliable, but do not convert general practice/mock/game attempts into delayed-recall successes. Their tasks, prompts, deadlines and prior disclosures differ. First release ingests qualifying success only from the new review protocol. A bank answer/discussion reveal outside it is not fully observable today: implement the narrow disclosure receipt where available, document the limit, and avoid claiming proof that the learner never saw a key elsewhere.

The strongest MCQ label is **“Retained in choices”**, with “Three spaced reviews answered correctly; next review …” supporting it. Other labels are **New**, **Learning**, **Building retention**, and **Due for review** as an independent due badge. Do not display “100% mastered”, an estimated probability of remembering, percentile, IQ/ability score or a new question difficulty label. Difficulty remains retired. The engine estimates what to review next; it does not measure a person's confidence.

## Deterministic scheduling policy v1

The following intervals and caps are product defaults to implement and evaluate, not scientifically fitted values or retention guarantees. Keep them in one versioned policy definition and pure transition function. Server time is authoritative for account schedules; UTC instants are stored, local dates displayed. Daily session quotas use the established Asia/Kolkata product boundary. Changing a timezone or browser clock cannot promote an account item.

Maintain a ladder index and a separate learning/relearning state. Recognition interval ladder: **1, 3, 7, 14, 30, 60 days**, capped at 60 days. Store `scheduled_at`, `due_at`, `last_exposure_at`, `last_qualifying_review_at`, consecutive delayed successes, lapse count, algorithm version and evidence version. Derive labels from these facts; avoid unexplained confidence numbers.

| Event | State and next review |
| --- | --- |
| New item's first clean correct answer | Enter Learning, index 0, next review in one day. No delayed success yet. |
| Correct due review, clean evidence | Advance at most one index and schedule that interval from receipt time, with at most one promotion per independent IST date. Increment delayed successes only when the time since last answer exposure meets the previously scheduled interval. Different dates alone do not qualify. A long absence never jumps several stages. |
| Incorrect or explicit answer reveal | Enter relearning, clear the consecutive success chain, reset index 0. Offer one review after at least ten minutes and three intervening distinct items; if the session ends, it remains due when the learner returns. |
| Correct relearning check | Schedule one day from this check, index 0. This checks immediate repair and does not count as a delayed success. |
| Second failure/reveal on that item in the same session | Show explanation and offer source/report/help; end same-session retries for it and schedule tomorrow. Do not trap the learner in an endless failure loop. |
| Voluntary correct practice before due | Record practice/exposure, leave the ladder unchanged, and set next review to no earlier than `max(old_due_at, now + current_interval)`. Start the evidence gap again after exposure; explain that the review date moved because it was practised early. |
| Skip | No correctness judgment, lapse or stage reset. Defer availability ten minutes and do not reveal the answer. If Skip also reveals an answer in the UI, record exposure separately and protect the next review gap. |
| Interrupted/invalid/ambiguous observation | No promotion or lapse; preserve the prior stage. Offer a fresh valid check. An expired delivery alone is not an answer. |

The early-practice rule avoids treating a successful review minutes after seeing a key as evidence retained over the preceding week. A wrong early practice still enters relearning. Do not let passive re-render/reload count as exposure to the answer: disclosure and actual checks are explicit events. Effective `last_exposure_at` includes trusted recent checked-answer disclosures on this canonical item when available.

“Retained in choices” requires at least **three consecutive qualifying delayed successes**, the latest after at least a seven-day exposure-free gap, with no unresolved content invalidation. For example: first correct today → correct tomorrow → correct three days later → correct seven days later meets the threshold. Correct five times today does not. Becoming overdue changes the due badge and queue priority, not historical answers into failures. A later lapse returns the item to Learning; the history remains visible.

Queue policy: enforce ten answered/revealed slots per session, including repair checks. Due items first, with at most two new items when fewer than ten due items exist; an initial new-user session may introduce up to ten. Stable order by due time then item ID; fresh selection can use application-side ID draws, never SQL `ORDER BY random()`. Do not reserve slots forever for a ten-minute retry: end with an honest next-review time if nothing else is ready. “Continue practising” starts a separate explicitly chosen session; it does not auto-refill the completed one. If the pool is small, allow a shorter session without unrelated/unverified substitutes. Suspension/unavailability preserves history but removes the item from usable due counts.

Cold start uses these defaults for everyone, without a proficiency/confidence questionnaire. Historical server attempts may mark “Previously practised”, but cannot establish the new delayed-success chain. Old manual schedules can seed a next check under the migration policy below. Do not punish overdue backlogs: show a manageable due-first batch and remaining count, with no escalating streak penalty.

## Latency and accessibility

Answer correctness, spacing and assistance are sufficient for the first complete engine. Do **not** make slower readers repeat more or let fast guessing accelerate mastery. Start no response timer until the complete accessible prompt is rendered; pause optional active-time measurement when hidden/unfocused or explicitly paused. Network round trips and time waiting for assets are not reading time. Idle thresholds cannot reliably distinguish reading, assistive technology, interruption and inactivity: mark timing unknown after a long idle period rather than interpreting it.

Timing collection should be optional/off initially and limited to a coarse duration bucket plus flags such as interrupted/unreliable, not keystrokes, pointer trails, audio, biometrics or disability inference. Server issue/receive times support token lifecycle, not claims of exact thinking time. If later analysis justifies a timing feature, compare only a learner's own reliable observations within the same task format; require enough data and a documented fallback. It must never override correctness, introduce speed points or require the user to reveal an accessibility need. That later optimization is not a prerequisite for removing confidence ratings.

## Recognition now, checked recall later

Keep MCQ recognition as the default because existing keys objectively check it. Reuse the shared answer layout and complete source presentation; arbitrary option shuffling is unsafe for “all of the above”, sequence, letter-referenced and formatted options. Do not shuffle merely to manufacture apparent novelty. A learner may mentally recall before reading choices, but the engine still records recognition.

A later typed-recall mode requires independently reviewed task metadata: precise prompt, sense/direction, canonical accepted answer(s), narrowly defined normalization, reviewed aliases and provenance. Automatically extracted question text or an official MCQ key does not establish every acceptable synonym. Check only reviewed alternatives; an unmatched plausible answer is **unassessed**, not automatically wrong. Offer the original MCQ and a correction/report route. Exact known matches can be marked correct; review-approved explicit wrong alternatives may be marked wrong; do not ask an LLM to certify open-ended correctness at runtime. Never reduce a learner's state because semantic validation is unavailable.

Typed tasks have separate evidence and labels from recognition; recognition can propose when to try recall, but cannot grant recall mastery. A future reviewed lexeme/sense graph may aggregate question evidence transparently. This is a separate content workflow, not a prerequisite for a useful automatic scheduler.

## Server contract, identity and correctness

Add a dedicated vocabulary router/service rather than making the browser submit a correctness/rating/stage field. Suggested endpoints: create/resume session, obtain current card, submit its answer/reveal/skip, read summary/history, and correct an accidental action. Legacy import is a later optional endpoint. Use existing session authentication, write/CSRF guards and answer-protection gates. New `/vocabulary` API calls must explicitly carry the current exam scope; the existing generic `scopedApiPath` matcher does not currently include that prefix. Readiness/status can be public, but private review records require `require_account`, never a forged device header.

Use additive numbered migrations with these minimal records:

| Record | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| `vocabulary_sessions` and card deliveries | Account owner, frozen exam selection/policy, ten-slot limit, active slot, bounded delivery expiry, snapshot/hash, unique request receipt and expected progress version. Fetch does not consume a slot or advance mastery. |
| `vocabulary_review_events` | Append-only observation ID, owner, canonical/task/version, session/token, chosen text or explicit reveal/skip, server correctness nullable, assistance/timing-quality flags, linked real attempt ID, policy version and source identity. No self-rated confidence. |
| `vocabulary_progress` | Rebuildable projection keyed by account + canonical + task + content version, due/state/version and event cursor; one indexed due lookup. It is user-owned, never bank-sync-owned. |
| Correction/import receipts | Actor, target event/import hash, reason, immutable before/after reference and request identity. Preserve originals; replay produces the current projection. |

Strict eligible content uses the selected scope's `v_scorable`, approved English vocabulary paths, actual paper origins and a matching source appearance with usable options/instructions/figures. Constrain subject/topic on the same reviewed path. Never fall back to derived/inferred answers or promote a personal tag into taxonomy. Use existing scope semantics: MPSC means MPSC plus Mizoram recruitment; All exams uses current global helper behavior, including its lack of an enabled-scope filter. Do not silently change that product-wide policy here. Source identity and the exact selected appearance are frozen on each delivery. Exam scope filters the queue, while the same canonical/task learning state is shared across scopes so switching scope cannot reset mastery or duplicate evidence. Ineligible/out-of-scope history remains accessible to its owner with an explanation.

Grade the delivered option mapping by text against the snapshot, with key/presentation/scope eligibility revalidated inside the same transaction as event, attempt, projection and receipt. If meaning, answer provenance, options or required source context changed, return “This question was updated; load the current version”, without scheduling a success/failure. Hold the relevant content row lock or equivalent revision predicate through commit to avoid a grading/publication race. Reuse existing active mock/game/cup answer protection **before** returning a prompt's key, hint or review. Do not call the generic submission endpoint and then write scheduling in a second transaction.

For an actual choice, insert exactly one ordinary `review` attempt using server-generated scoring/source proof. For reveal/skip there is no fabricated chosen answer or boolean attempt. A unique `(owner,delivery_id)` receipt returns the same response on retry; conflicting choices for an already-used token fail. Serialize account session allocation and item projection updates, lock in a documented order, and use expected versions. Two tabs may resume the same active session/card; they cannot earn two transitions for it. A per-account/per-scope card constraint still requires a shared per-account/per-canonical projection lock across scopes. Different expired/stale deliveries for the same item cannot leapfrog versions. Freeze the current card in React until its acknowledged transition; do not let periodic due recounts change it under the answer controls.

Account schedules sync on next fetch across devices using server state. Use private/no-store responses, account-bound caches and generation guards on auth and exam-scope changes, including the planned smooth SPA scope switch. Capture the active card's scope in its request; switching the global preference must not grade it with a new scope or attach its late response to a new card. A response lost after commit must recover its receipt; Next may navigate only after acknowledgement. Offline account responses remain “Not saved”; do not show a new mastery state or schedule based on unacknowledged client timestamps. First release requires connectivity for grading instead of introducing an offline merge protocol.

## Guest continuity, existing ratings and error correction

The first release asks guests to sign in free to use automatic scheduling, explains that it syncs their review dates, and links to ordinary English practice as an alternative. Do not leave a required confidence-rating fallback in the main Vocabulary journey. Private events/history require an account; reset in-memory state on logout and never show the previous account's summary in the guest view. Browser-local automatic scheduling can be evaluated later, with a shared transition contract; it is not required for this first engine.

For v1, **leave `ism:vocabulary-recall:v1` untouched and do not import it**. Show a one-time explanation that automatic reviews start from checked answers and old browser review data has been preserved. Historical server attempts may mark previously practised items but never grant the new delayed-success chain. Do not read the local map into new accuracy/mastery totals. Rollback may reopen the old browser experience, so avoid deleting or rewriting its storage format.

If a later import is justified, treat that payload as an untrusted aggregate, not a list of ratings. Parse with bounded size, valid IDs, finite dates and bounded counters; preserve the raw local backup until a successful explicit import. Offer once: **“Use this browser's old review dates?”** with account identity and a warning that shared-browser history has no known owner. Import only opted-in valid item dates as `legacy_manual`, schedule a first automatic check no later than seven days from import (past-due stays due), and derive **no** automatic successes from `seen`, `right` or `stage`. Old aggregates remain “Legacy self-rated reviews”, not new correctness statistics.

Any future import must fingerprint the input per account, deduplicate retries and preserve newer verified automatic progress. No blind localStorage upload on sign-in, retrospective rewards or cross-account import through the existing automatic attempt-adoption loop. Existing old clients may continue writing their local v1 map, but cannot mutate new server progress. An old server `review` attempt without a delivery receipt remains ordinary historical practice, never a reconstructed clean recall event.

After feedback, offer optional **“Accidental tap”**. It appends a scheduling-invalidation event and rebuilds the affected projection under lock; it does not rewrite the original chosen answer, turn wrong into correct or remove a reward record. Repeated requests return the same correction receipt. The disclosed answer remains an exposure, so the next check cannot immediately promote the prior stage. “Review sooner” is an optional scheduling preference, not confidence evidence; it may shorten a due date without changing mastery. User reports about a wrong key use the existing reviewed correction workflow and can suspend the item locally pending review.

Bank corrections/withdrawals require a version invalidation path before launch: new delivery and due reads detect source/content changes lazily, while existing publication/reassessment signals can enqueue bounded refresh work where available. Preserve original attempts/events; exclude invalid evidence and rebuild the scheduling projection. Cosmetic label changes need not erase learning; meaning/key/presentation changes do. If historical comparability cannot be established, mark the new version “Needs a fresh check” rather than inventing a conversion. User reports never change an official key directly.

## Learner flow and privacy

The normal flow is **Start reviews → choose an answer → Check answer → feedback and next review date → Next**. Scheduling is saved as part of Check; Next is navigation. Copy: “We'll choose when to review this again from your answers over time.” Show “Correct choice”, the verified answer/explanation, “Review tomorrow” and a short reason such as “First check; we'll check again after a gap.” Do not ask how confident the learner felt. Include “I don't know — show answer” and Skip without forcing a guess.

Dashboard shows usable due count, items introduced, recent first-answer accuracy with denominator, and retained-recognition count. Keep reveals/skips separate from answer accuracy. Show zero accuracy as 0%, not a dash; use a dash only with no checked answers. Handle loading, genuinely empty eligible pool, unavailable/source-changed items, no due items and network error as distinct states. Show the selected exam scope and next due date. A completed session ends after ten consumed slots and summarizes learning actions without awarding new XP.

Reuse warm product styling, SourceQuestionContext/SourceQuestionText and AnswerOptions. Preserve keyboard selection, visible focus, screen-reader feedback, 44px controls, source links, readable wrapping and a real 360px layout. Announce saved/error status without reading timers continuously. No response deadline, speed leaderboard, mandatory animation or streak-loss warning. Do not add email/push reminders, study-partner sharing, public mastery rankings or difficulty controls.

Keep schedules private to the authenticated account, with explicit export/reset controls and a documented deletion path. Reset appends a new scheduling epoch and does not erase bank attempts or reward accounting accidentally. Minimize stored response data to existing chosen-answer proof and scheduling evidence; do not persist raw typed free text in the first MCQ release. No provider receives learner answer histories because the scheduler is local application code. Do not claim anonymous/device storage is protected like an authenticated account.

Define retention before expanding telemetry: optional coarse timing diagnostics may expire after 30 days; retain only minimal review evidence needed for explainable scheduling while the learner keeps that history. Account deletion/export must include these new tables, projections, receipts and backups according to the product's actual policy; full account erasure is not currently established by this audit. Do not create immutable triggers without an explicit authorized deletion route. Aggregate evaluation must not expose individual due lists or require new identifying data.

## Research basis and limits

Repeated retrieval and spacing are reasonable foundations; they do not establish the numerical intervals above or let an MCQ expose a learner's internal certainty. The [Karpicke and Roediger retrieval experiment](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408) motivates checking again after initial learning. [Tabibian et al.'s spaced-repetition study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6410796/) demonstrates modeling review outcomes and timing, with model assumptions and fitted parameters. [Duolingo's original half-life regression implementation](https://github.com/duolingo/halflife-regression) illustrates the data/evaluation needed for learned recall predictions. The practical recommendation here is an engineering inference: begin with an auditable evidence-driven policy, collect the necessary delayed observations, and defer numerical mastery probabilities until they can be calibrated for this product's tasks.
